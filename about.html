Our Mission

The Farmer's Basin makes the uneven reality of water security across the Murray-Darling Basin visible. We bring water availability, allocation reliability and regional agriculture together on one interactive map, so the people shaping water policy can see who is most exposed and why.

8,400 irrigated agricultural businesses rely on the Basin
2000 – 2021 years of catchment-level water market data behind the map

Why We Built It

A farmer's access to water depends on where they farm, what kind of entitlement they hold, each year's allocation decisions and the season they're facing. General-security holders, concentrated in NSW catchments such as the Murrumbidgee, are usually the first and hardest hit in dry years, while high-security holders downstream are rarely affected. That information is spread across many datasets, which makes the difference between regions hard to see from a policy desk. We think water insecurity in the Basin is shaped by policy design as much as by geography or drought, and that it needs to be seen to be addressed.

What You Can Do Here

Explore the Basin

Select a region on the map to see its water availability, allocation reliability and the agriculture that depends on it.

Meet Farmer Personas

Read the Economics

Voices from the Basin

Behind every allocation figure is a community. These regional organisations show how differently water policy is felt across the Basin.

Gwydir, NSW

Gwydir Valley Irrigators Association

Reports that only around 19% of long-term river flows remain available for irrigation in the Gwydir, following water recovery under the Basin Plan.

Riverland, SA

Central Irrigation Trust

Shows how water trading lets high-security regions sustain permanent horticulture by buying entitlements from elsewhere in the Basin.

Cowra, NSW

Mid Lachlan Landcare

Runs farmer-inclusive sustainability projects, showing that farming and environmental recovery don't have to be at odds.

Who It's For

Policymakers and government officers

People working in water management, agriculture and regional planning who need a clear, no-GIS-required view of how decisions land in different regions.

Basin communities and stakeholders

The Murray-Darling Basin Authority, regional and local governments, agricultural organisations and farmers who live with these decisions and hold the local knowledge.
